aaron

I am a builder and an adventurer. I make things for people who are starting over. The parts I care about are the ones that have to stay clear, quick, and honest while a life is moving.

scroll

2023 – now

A country, as software.

At Issa Compass I get to work on the problem I wanted: global immigration, as software. Visas, work permits, the fifty hours of forms that used to take nine months and three thousand dollars. We started in a wet market. Two weeks later Zin, a mathematician packing fish until dawn, had a restaurant job and three times the pay. The rest of the company is that, repeated.

I also serve as a visiting partner at Iterative, a Y Combinator-style program for Southeast Asia. Since 2021 they have helped more than sixty-five companies grow from pre-seed to a collective $1.2 billion.

2021 – 2022

A language is a border.

In Medellín I helped Viva put live translation into meetings, across twenty countries, for people who would otherwise not be in the room. The waitlist hit a hundred thousand in a month. I learned that the hardest part of moving is often the conversation that has to happen first.

2019 – 2021

Who gets a place.

At Social Construct, Y Combinator’s New Cities lab, I ran the software that took a building from a sketch to a supply schedule to the robots that would put it up. In the same years I co-founded OutBrave, to aid refugees, and helped raise tens of millions for partners who would never look like a startup. Both jobs were about who is allowed to stay. There were other adventures. These are the ones that mattered.

2015 – 2019

A room you could walk around.

Houzz bought the company. I stayed and helped start Visual Technologies, its moonshot lab: AR and VR for how people imagine a home. I guest lectured at Stanford on the same problems. The work that survives a sale is the work you still want to do when the logo changes.

2014 – 2015

Starting from a camera.

At twenty-three I founded Roominnate, a computer vision startup that reconstructed interiors in three dimensions. In six months we had pilots with Facebook, Microsoft, Valve, and a handful of Fortune 500s. We sold to Houzz so the thing would reach families instead of remaining a demo. I learned that an exit is a decision about who the work is for.

2013 – 2014

Games, then the layer underneath.

I started in games: algorithms for an AMC title that twenty million people played, then Big Fish, where I moved from gameplay to a native SDK that shipped the same thing to web, phone, and desktop for more than a hundred million. The company later sold for nearly a billion. What I kept was the habit of shipping to people who will never know your name.

before

A carpet mill in Georgia.

My father was a street orphan in Hong Kong. He moved to rural Georgia to work in a mill. My mother worked next to him. I worked three jobs through Georgia Tech and mopped floors for a room. Everything after that is their work, continuing. I build for people who are doing what they did.
