What’s the most important problem you could be working on right now?

scroll

2023 – now

A country, as software.

At Issa Compass we are building the missing layer: a system that turns immigration into something you can actually act on. The rules exist. They have never been organized. Applicants don’t know which visa they qualify for. Small errors get you rejected. We treat it as a sequence of decisions, not a pile of documents. Eligibility, then the process, then the forms, in that order. A five-year visa approved in under twelve hours. That is what happens when the process behaves like software.

I also serve as a Visiting Partner at Iterative, the Y Combinator-style program for Southeast Asia. Since 2021 they have taken more than sixty-five companies from pre-seed to a collective $1.2 billion.

2021 – 2022

A language is a border.

At Viva we treated language as a border and tried to delete it. Live translation in meetings across twenty countries, so the people who belong in the room can actually be in it. A hundred thousand on the waitlist in a month. Moving countries is hard. Being unable to speak is often the part that stops you.

2019 – 2021

A home for anyone who wants one.

At Social Construct, Y Combinator’s New Cities lab, we tried to make housing cheap enough that inclusion is just what the building costs. I ran the software from a sketch of a building to the schedule that ordered its parts to the robots that put them up.

In the same years I founded OutBrave to help refugees, and we raised tens of millions for work that will never look like a startup. Same bet: the world has room. We just haven’t built it yet.

2015 – 2019

Walk through a house that isn’t built.

Houzz acquired Roominnate, the computer vision company I started at twenty-three. I stayed and built Visual Technologies, their moonshot lab, so you could walk through a home before a nail went in. An acquisition is only interesting if you keep building.

I guest lectured at Stanford on the same problem.

2014 – 2015

Starting from a camera.

I founded Roominnate at twenty-three to reconstruct interiors in 3D from a photograph. Six months in we had pilots with Facebook, Microsoft, Valve, and a handful of Fortune 500s. We sold to Houzz so families would get it, not a slide deck. That is the only reason to sell.

before

A carpet mill in Georgia.

My father was a street orphan in Hong Kong. He moved to rural Georgia to work in a mill. My mother worked next to him. I worked three jobs through Georgia Tech and mopped floors for a room. Everything since is a continuation of that. I build for people doing what they did.
