Aaron

I care about a better world. A free one, for all of us. To do that, I build software with scale, speed, and authenticity.

What’s the most important problem you could be working on right now?

scroll

2023 – now

A country, as software.

At Issa Compass we’re turning disorganized immigration rules into deterministic, repeatable process. And magic happens. We got a five-year visa approved in under twelve hours. Immigration at the speed of software.

I also serve as a Visiting Partner at Iterative, the Y Combinator-style program for Southeast Asia. Over sixty-five companies from pre-seed to a combined $1.2 billion valuation since 2021.

2021 – 2022

A language is a border.

At Viva we treated language as a border and imagined a borderless world. Live translation in meetings across twenty countries, so the people who belong in the room can actually be in it. I scaled us to a hundred thousand on the waitlist in a month. Moving countries is hard, but being unable to speak is often the part that stops you.

2019 – 2021

A home for anyone who wants one.

At Social Construct, Y Combinator’s New Cities lab, we redesigned housing costs to behave like Moore’s Law: exponentially cheaper until housing is universally accessible for everyone. I ran the software from a sketch of a building to the schedule that ordered its parts to the robots that installed them.

In the same years I founded OutBrave to help refugees. We served as a startup studio for humanitarian work, raised tens of millions to alleviate housing and mobility challenges. The world has room. We just haven’t built it yet.

2015 – 2019

The future of interior design.

Houzz acquired Roominnate, the computer vision company I started at twenty-three. I stayed and built Visual Technologies, their moonshot lab, so you could walk through the house that yours will become. We won Google’s App of the Year. An acquisition is only interesting if you keep building.

I guest lectured at Stanford University on design and entrepreneurship.

2014 – 2015

Starting from a camera.

Soon after graduation, I founded Roominnate to reconstruct interiors in 3D from a photograph. Six months in we had pilots with Facebook, Microsoft, Valve, and a handful of Fortune 500s. We sold to Houzz to give our technology to millions of families, sooner.

before

A carpet mill in Georgia.

My father was a street orphan in Hong Kong who ended up working a mill floor in rural Georgia. My mother worked the line next to him. I put myself through Georgia Tech on three jobs at once. This is the family business: taking nothing and building something. I’m just doing it at global scale now.
