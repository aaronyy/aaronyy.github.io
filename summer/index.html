    Eleven Thousand Summers

    Twelve thousand years ago the ice let go, and people began to stay put. This is what happened next, on one map. The glow is where people were, from a few million foragers to eight billion; the colours are the civilizations they belonged to. Hover or tap anything for its story.

    • ice, sheets and September sea ice
    • people, coloured by civilization
    • cities, from their first record
    • people on the move
    • trade routes
    • inventions, cables, satellites, data centres
    • turning points: climate, plagues, empires, ideas, eruptions

    The cards are the turning points: droughts and plagues, empires rising and falling, voyages, revolutions, and the few eruptions that changed the weather. Every VEI 6 eruption in the Smithsonian's record also bursts on the map, sharp where someone saw it happen, smoky where it was reconstructed from ash and ice. ?vei=4 shows the smaller ones.

    The timeline opens at the last glacial maximum, 21,000 BCE, with the ice at its fullest and no one yet on the map. Play through it and the view flies in from beyond the Moon and unfolds the globe into the map; the story begins at 10,000 BCE, where the population record does. The lines on the way in, and on the closing card, are Carl Sagan's, from Pale Blue Dot (1994).

    The civilizations, with their dates, extents and colours, are drawn by hand and approximate. This is a way of seeing the record, not a substitute for it.

    Data

    • Smithsonian Institution, Global Volcanism Program, Volcanoes of the World, Holocene eruption list.
    • Cities: Reba, Reitsma & Seto (2016), digitised from Chandler and Modelski, CC BY 4.0.
    • Data centres: hand-curated from public announcements, illustrative. Data-centre power from the IEA.
    • Ice sheets: ICE-6G_C, Peltier, Argus & Drummond (2015).
    • Sea ice: NSIDC Sea Ice Index, Version 4, September Arctic extent; before 1979 the 1980 extent stands in.
    • HYDE 3.3, Klein Goldewijk et al., PBL Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency and Utrecht University, CC BY 3.0.
    • Coastlines and shaded relief: Natural Earth, public domain.
    • Satellites: scaled to cumulative launches from UNOOSA's index of objects launched into outer space. Cables and milestones are hand-written.
    • Civilization table, milestones, turning points and the notes on cities and cables are hand-written and approximate. Before 1500 the table lists the major cultural complexes with a placeable heartland; from 1500 on, the polities that held the most people in their era.
